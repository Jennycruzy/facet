How Facet protects you

Full detail

Your wallet is not the app's wallet

Facet pays an app from a private balance instead of sending money straight from your main wallet. The app sees a separate account, not the wallet that funded the balance.

Common amounts leave fewer clues

The SDK can enforce shared amounts when money enters the private pool. The current Mainnet launcher does not provide that funding step; app spending remains user-selected.

Timing can give people a clue

If two accounts are funded and used seconds apart, people can guess they belong together. The current launcher does not space actions for you, so timing is still your responsibility.

Facet stops the easiest connections

A protected route refuses public recipients that match addresses you supplied as linked. The current Ekubo and Endur helper routes expose no user-selected public recipient.

You approve every live action

For the live Mainnet routes, your connected wallet shows you what will happen and asks for your approval. Facet never receives your private key.

What nothing can hide

Inherent, not a to-do list

The app can see the account

Once an account acts, its address, actions, balances, and timing are public. Facet hides the link back to your private balance, not the app activity.

You can still link accounts yourself

Moving money between your accounts, reusing one account across apps, or repeating an obvious pattern can reveal that they belong together. Facet cannot hide a link you create in public.
